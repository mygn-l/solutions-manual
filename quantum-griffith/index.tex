\subsection*{Problem 1.3}
(a)
Since it's uniform, we want area of rectangle $\pi \rho = 1 \quad \Rightarrow \rho = \frac{1}{\pi}$ for $\theta \in [0, \pi]$. Obviously, $\rho = 0$ for $\theta \in [-\frac{\pi}{2}, 0) \cup (\pi, \frac{3\pi}{2}]$.

\noindent
(b)
$$\langle \theta \rangle = \frac{\pi}{2}$$
$$\langle \theta^2 \rangle = \int_0^\pi \theta^2 \rho(\theta) d\theta = \int_0^\pi \theta^2 \frac{1}{\pi} d\theta = \frac{1}{3\pi} \left[ \theta^3 \right]_0^\pi = \frac{\pi^2}{3}$$

\noindent
(c)
$$\langle \sin \theta \rangle = \int_0^\pi \sin \theta \frac{1}{\pi} d\theta = -\frac{1}{\pi} \left[ \cos \theta \right]_0^\pi = \frac{2}{\pi}$$
Cosine is odd around $\theta = \frac{\pi}{2}$. So $\langle \cos \theta \rangle = 0$.
$$\langle \cos^2 \theta \rangle = \int_0^\pi \cos^2 \theta \frac{1}{\pi} d\theta = \frac{1}{\pi} \left[ \frac{\theta}{2} + \frac{\sin 2\theta}{4} \right]_0^\pi = \frac{1}{2}$$
